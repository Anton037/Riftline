# Riftline full-game quality audit prompt

## Assignment

Audit Riftline as if you were deciding whether it feels finished enough to ship. Find and fix anything broken, incomplete, misleading, awkward, or cheaply assembled. A feature can pass a functional test and still fail this audit if a player cannot tell what happened, an animation gives the wrong impression, or a common action feels clumsy. Look for omissions and inconsistencies as well as crashes. The examples below show how closely to look. Apply the same standard to every game system.

Read the current branch, release notes, implementation plan if present, and existing verification results. Inventory the current screens, settings, actions, weapons, abilities, enemies, bosses, biomes, hazards, rewards, progression modes, and save paths. Make a coverage table with a row for each area and mark it verified, fixed and retested, or unverified with a reason. Treat the inventory as a guard against skipping less visible systems. Inspect what players actually see, hear, and control in a rendered build before relying on implementation details or existing assertions.

Keep Riftline's cozy storybook direction and use simple, familiar names. Preserve content IDs, save compatibility, and deterministic seeds. Use keyboard and mouse on desktop and landscape touch on phones and tablets. Keep controller support and new accessibility settings outside the scope. You may improve or replace a model, pose, animation, effect, cue, flow, or piece of copy when it makes the game clearer or more satisfying. Confirm the gain at the camera distance and screen size used during play.

## Review every interaction from input to feedback

- Follow each player action through input, game rules, visible response, sound, and saved state where applicable. Check normal use, mistakes, cancellation, rapid repetition, simultaneous inputs, transitions, and recovery. Look for missed taps, stuck controls, clicks that leak onto the next screen, duplicate actions, and effects left behind from a previous run.
- On desktop, sweep the cursor around the Keeper. While the cursor controls aim, the head, chest, stance, weapon, and aiming cues should turn together. Check idle aim, firing, strafing, backing away, Auto Fire, rapid 180-degree turns, dash, and the handoff between manual and automatic aim. Pay attention to what the player sees between two settled poses.
- Inspect the Keeper at normal gameplay scale, with all seven weapons and from the front, side, and back. Check whether the face and silhouette make facing clear without using the gun, aim line, or ground marker as a clue. Look for limbs, cloak, hat, and weapon clipping, floating grips, recoil that detaches a gun, and parts that twist or reset late. If the model hides the facing cue, change the shapes or contrast and compare before and after at gameplay scale.
- Trace every shot and its flash, trail, sound, and impact from the actual muzzle. Check point-blank hits, cover at the player's shoulder and at the barrel, secondary and returning shots, fast turns, recoil, and crouched or dashing poses. Check that enemies give readable windups and that directional cues point toward their source.
- Test new touch controls at short landscape sizes and on tablets. Hold one stick while aiming, dashing, pausing or taking a reward with another finger. Try small and long drags, reversals, missed taps, offscreen cards, and rotation or backgrounding while a finger is down. Check both movement stick modes and the button layout editor's preview, save, cancel, and reset.

## Walk the whole game

- Start with a clean save. Browse every menu. Try the first launch, tutorial, first wave, first craft, first boss, Roadside Forge, victory, Endless, death, retry, abandon, and return home. Repeat the relevant paths with an existing save and a late run. Check every button, setting, toggle, and confirmation from its visible entry point through its actual effect. Remove stale copy, dead controls, and screens that offer a choice with no consequence.
- Watch combat from the player's point of view across all weapons, abilities, enemy roles, bosses, biomes, hazards, and wave events. Check target acquisition, hitboxes, cooldowns, invulnerability, damage numbers, hitstop, phase changes, and rewards. Verify that the safest response can be understood from the warning and that hits feel earned.
- Review recipes, upgrades, rerolls, Seal, shops, workshop upgrades, trophies, and progression. Check whether every offer can help the current build, prices and rewards make sense, benefits match their descriptions, and the game can continue when no options remain. Compare a weak and a strong build, ordinary and boss waves, and Endless around wave 40 and well past it.
- Inspect the HUD, cards, overlays, menus, arena dressing, lighting, particles, and animations at several sizes and in busy scenes. Look for clipped text, unreadable cues, overlapping buttons, pop-in, flicker, z-fighting, foot sliding, visual noise, delayed feedback, and repeated tutorial text. Listen for missing, repeated, overly loud, inaudible, cut-off, or lingering sounds. Check that area and boss tracks balance at the same volume.
- When something feels off, isolate the cause. Reproduce it at normal speed and inspect a slow capture or frame-by-frame view when needed. Compare the visible result before and after the change. Prefer changes that solve a specific problem. Suggest new features only when they address a clear gap in play.

## Stress performance, persistence, and stability

- Measure startup to usable controls, frame pacing, and CPU time in a steady scene and around transitions. Use equivalent seeds, viewports, frame caps, and rendering conditions. Measure the home screen, first fight, crowded Endless wave, boss, immediate reward pick, area change, pause, and resume. Record median, high-percentile, and worst frames with renderer and device details. Treat software rendering and device performance as different measurements.
- Watch for stalls from shader or texture creation, per-frame allocations or layout reads, growing entity, particle, or sound pools, and work that continues under menus. Repeat restarts, resizing, and backgrounding. Check sleep, offline operation, audio suspension, WebGL loss and recovery, and stale input after focus changes.
- Verify automatic checkpoints, old and partial saves, backup import and export, invalid data, storage failures, two open tabs, and leaving during a wave transition or immediately after defeat. Test an offline reload after a real update. Confirm that the version is coherent and the exact offer, purchase, or run state resumes as intended. Review untrusted input paths, dependency advisories, worker cache integrity, and release permissions defensively.

## Finish with evidence

For each finding record the player-visible symptom, device and build, reproduction steps or seed and wave, cause, fix, and verification. Use real controls whenever possible and make test fixtures explicit. Add a focused regression for bugs the existing suite missed. Recheck nearby behaviors and compare rendered frames for visual changes. Report systems you couldn't exercise, with the reason. State which checks used emulation and which still need a physical device.

Update the in-game What's New for significant changes. Run `npm run format && npm run check` with `PLAYWRIGHT_CHROMIUM_EXECUTABLE_PATH=/usr/bin/google-chrome` before every push. Run browser tests and probes one at a time. Verify the Pages deployment, build identity, and a live smoke test.
